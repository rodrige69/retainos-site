How to Fill Empty Salon Suites and Keep Your Renters Longer

Quick answer

To fill empty salon suites and keep renters longer, give new renters a reason to sign now that doesn't cut your rent (like a complimentary getaway or hotel savings card), reward current renters who refer a pro who signs, remind and reward renters before their lease ends, and help renters stay busy with a loyalty program for their own clients. Busy renters renew.

An empty salon suite earns nothing, and every week it sits empty is income you don't get back. Most owners reach for the same fix: a month of free rent. There are better tools.

Why Salon Suites Sit Empty

Suite renters are independent business owners. They choose a building for location, price and how it feels, and they leave for the same reasons. The most common one is simple: their book slows down. When a stylist, esthetician or lash artist stops covering rent comfortably, they move to a cheaper suite or go back to a commission salon.

That means filling suites and keeping them full are two different jobs. Leasing brings renters in. A renter's own business keeps them there.

The Problem With Free Rent

A month of free rent is easy to advertise, but look at the math. On a suite that rents for $1,400 a month, one free month costs you $1,400 on every new lease, whether the renter stays a year or leaves after three months. It also sets a price expectation: the next prospect asks for the same deal, and current renters notice.

Concessions aren't always wrong. But they should be one option, not the only one.

1. Offer a Lease-Signing Reward Instead of Free Rent

A lease-signing reward gives a prospect a reason to say yes this week without lowering your rent. Options include a complimentary getaway certificate, a hotel savings card, or a building perk like a month of upgraded storage.

Make the reward earned, not promised: issue it after the lease is signed and the first payment clears. Put the condition in writing on your leasing page and in the application.

Be clear about what the reward is. A hotel savings card is a discount or credit on eligible hotel bookings, not cash or a prepaid stay. A getaway certificate usually requires the guest to pay taxes and fees when activating. Say so upfront.

2. Turn Renters Into Your Leasing Team

Your best leads already work in your building. Stylists know other stylists, and a pro who is happy in your building is more convincing than any listing.

A renter referral program can be simple: when a current renter refers a pro who signs a lease and pays, both get a reward. Give renters a short link or code to share, and verify the lease before issuing anything. Don't count a tour or an application as a referral.

3. Start Renewal Conversations Early

Most renters decide whether to renew well before the lease ends. If the first conversation happens the week it expires, you may already be too late.

About 60 days out, send a friendly reminder with the renewal date and a thank-you for renewing early, like a hotel savings card issued once the renewal is signed. A reminder sent on time does more than a counteroffer sent after the renter has toured another building.

4. Help Renters Stay Busy

This is the step most buildings skip, and the one that matters most. A renter with a full book pays on time and renews. A renter with gaps starts looking around.

You can't do a renter's marketing for them, but you can give them tools. Some suite buildings now include a loyalty and referral program for each renter's own clients as an amenity, next to Wi-Fi and laundry. Renters get QR check-in, visit rewards, referral tracking and rebooking reminders under their own business name. Their client list stays theirs.

It helps you in two ways: busier renters stay longer, and a renter whose rebooking system runs out of your building has one more reason not to move.

5. Measure Occupancy and Renewals, Not Just Leads

Track a few numbers every month:

  • Occupancy rate = occupied suites ÷ total suites × 100
  • Renewal rate = leases renewed ÷ leases that came up for renewal × 100
  • Average days vacant per suite turnover
  • Leasing cost per signed lease, including concessions, rewards and listings

If occupancy is high but renewals are low, focus on steps 3 and 4. If renewals are strong but vacancies drag on, focus on steps 1 and 2.

Worked Example (Illustrative)

Illustrative example: A 30-suite building rents suites at $1,400 a month and usually turns over 8 suites a year. Offering one free month on each new lease would cost 8 × $1,400 = $11,200 a year in lost rent. If the owner instead offers a lease-signing reward and a renewal reward, the question becomes how much those rewards and the program cost per lease compared with $1,400. Every suite that renews instead of turning over also avoids the vacant weeks between renters. These figures are hypothetical, not industry averages or RetainOS results.

Sample Lease Reward Terms

Eligibility: New renters who sign a lease of 12 months or longer. When earned: After the lease is signed and the first payment clears. Renewal reward: Earned when a renewal is signed before the date in your reminder. Referral reward: Earned by both the current renter and the new renter after the referred renter signs and pays. Exclusions: Applications, tours and canceled leases don't qualify. Reward: Not cash, rent credit or a gift card. Reward terms and conditions apply.

This is a starting example, not legal advice. Check your lease, any franchise agreement, and with your accountant on how incentives are recorded.

A 30-Day Plan for Suite Owners

Week 1: Choose one lease-signing reward and one renewal reward. Write the rules. Week 2: Add the offer to your leasing page and application, and list every lease ending in the next 90 days. Week 3: Launch renter referrals with a simple link or code, and send renewal reminders to anyone within 60 days. Week 4: Offer the loyalty perk to current renters, then review occupancy, renewals and days vacant.

Frequently asked questions

How do I fill empty salon suites fast?

Give prospects a reason to sign now that doesn't lower your rent, like a lease-signing reward, and ask current renters to refer pros they know. Make sure your listing shows the amenities renters care about.

Is free rent a good way to fill salon suites?

It works short term, but it costs a full month of income on every lease and sets an expectation for the next renter. A lease-signing reward is often cheaper and feels bigger to the renter.

How do I keep salon suite renters from leaving?

Start renewal conversations about 60 days early, thank renters for renewing, and help them keep their books full. Renters whose businesses are growing rarely move.

What is a good amenity for salon suite renters?

Beyond Wi-Fi, laundry and storage, a loyalty and referral program for each renter's own clients helps renters stay busy, which helps them pay rent and renew.

Can renters earn rewards for referring other renters?

Yes. A common rule is that both the current renter and the new renter get a reward after the new renter signs a lease and pays.

Do lease rewards replace concessions entirely?

Not always. Some markets still expect concessions. Rewards give you another tool, so you don't have to lead with discounts.
